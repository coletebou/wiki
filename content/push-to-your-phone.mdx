Pushing straight to your phone means running your own build on your own iPhone, directly from [[xcode|Xcode]], with
no TestFlight and no App Store in between. It is the tightest loop in [[ios-development]]: change a line, press
Run, and the app is on the phone a few seconds later with the debugger attached. You can do it with a free Apple
Account, within limits; this page covers the setup, those limits, the command line version that agents use, and the
two ways to reach a phone without a cable.

## The quick version

1. **Sign in to Xcode.** Xcode, Settings, Apple Accounts. A free account shows up as a *Personal Team*.
2. **Set up signing.** In your app target's Signing and Capabilities tab, turn on "Automatically manage signing,"
   pick your team and give the app a unique bundle ID (for example `com.yourname.hello`).
3. **Pair the phone.** Connect it with a cable and tap Trust on the phone's "Trust This Computer" dialog. With iOS 27
   and Xcode 27 you can skip the cable: open Device Hub, choose the add button, then "Pair Nearby Device…" while the
   phone is on the same Wi-Fi.
4. **Turn on Developer Mode** on the phone (next section).
5. **Run.** Choose the phone as the run destination and press <kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd>. The first time, Xcode
   registers the device and creates a development profile for it.

Plug in once, trust the Mac, flip on Developer Mode, and from then on it's just Command-R. With a phone on iOS 27 you
don't even need the cable anymore.

## Developer Mode

Developer Mode is the switch that lets an iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch or Vision Pro run apps signed for development.
Apple's reason: it "protects people from inadvertently installing potentially harmful software." It does not affect
apps from the App Store or [[testflight|TestFlight]]; it is needed only for builds you run from Xcode or `.ipa` files
you install with Apple Configurator.

- On the phone, open Settings, Privacy and Security, and turn on Developer Mode under Security. The phone warns that
  it reduces security and asks to restart.
- After the restart, confirm with Enable and your passcode.
- The switch appears only once you have started pairing the device with a Mac (or paired it before), so a brand new
  phone will not show it until you plug it in or pair it.
- For an Apple Watch, turn it on on both the watch and its paired iPhone (see [[apple-watch-apps]]).

To turn it off, flip the same switch and restart; Xcode can no longer run apps on the device until you turn it back
on.

## The free account and its limits

A Personal Team costs nothing and is enough for building on your own phone. Apple lists the limits:

- Up to 10 App IDs and 3 devices, each expiring after 7 days.
- Up to 3 of your apps installed on a device at once.
- Provisioning profiles expire 7 days after they are issued, so the app stops launching after a week and you rebuild
  and reinstall it.
- No push notifications, iCloud, Sign in with Apple, Associated Domains or Apple Pay. App Groups, Keychain Sharing,
  HealthKit and background modes do work.

The first launch of a free-signed app can stop at an "Untrusted Developer" alert. Trust your developer certificate
once in Settings, General, VPN and Device Management, and it launches normally after that.

The paid Apple Developer Program (<Price id="apple-developer-program" />) removes the weekly expiry and the
capability gaps, and lets you register up to 100 devices of each kind per membership year.

## Without the cable

Xcode 27 pairs iPhones and iPads running iOS 27 over the network, and Apple calls it "a more reliable experience for
pairing watches" too. For phones on older iOS versions, pair with a cable once; after that, Apple's docs say you "can
disconnect the physical cable and run apps on the device through Xcode using Wi-Fi with IPv6 enabled on the same
network as your Mac."

A wireless pairing does not survive an operating system upgrade: after updating the phone, pair it again. And if the
Trust dialog was dismissed by accident or the device never shows up, Apple's advice is to restart the device.

## From the command line

Agents in [[tmux]] sessions cannot click Run, so they use `devicectl`, Apple's command line tool for physical devices,
which ships with Xcode. (Older guides use the third-party `ios-deploy`, which does not support iOS 17 and later.)

```sh
xcrun devicectl list devices                     # paired devices and their identifiers

xcodebuild -scheme MyApp -destination 'generic/platform=iOS' \
  -derivedDataPath build -allowProvisioningUpdates build

xcrun devicectl device install app --device <device-id> build/Build/Products/Debug-iphoneos/MyApp.app
xcrun devicectl device process launch --device <device-id> com.yourname.hello
```

`-allowProvisioningUpdates` lets `xcodebuild` register the device and refresh profiles the way the Xcode window does;
it is fine on your own Mac, and the wrong thing in CI, where it creates certificates on every clean runner. Add
`--json-output <file>` to a `devicectl` command to get results a script can parse; Xcode 27 can also send that JSON to
standard output (`devicectl --help` has the details).

That is how Cole's agents check a build on real hardware: install it with `devicectl`, launch it, and confirm on the
device that the right bundle, version and build number are installed and the process is running. A successful install
proves exactly that much. Signing in, the camera, location and push still need a human with the phone in hand.

## Ad hoc builds

An ad hoc build is a release-signed build that runs on a fixed list of registered devices, with no TestFlight and no
review. It needs the paid program, an App ID, a distribution certificate and an ad hoc provisioning profile that
names the devices; with automatic signing, Xcode manages that profile for you.

The limit is the device list: up to 100 devices per product family (iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Mac and so on) per
membership year. Disabling a device during the year does not give its slot back; you can remove devices and restore
the count to 100 only at the start of a new membership year. For most teams, TestFlight's internal testing does the
same job with less bookkeeping.

## TestFlight: the cable-free route

When the Mac that builds is not next to the phone, or a build comes out of CI, internal [[testflight|TestFlight]]
testing is the practical way to push to your phone. Internal builds skip Beta App Review, and with automatic
distribution on, a build reaches every phone in the internal group a few minutes after it finishes processing. The
trade-off is the upload and processing time, and no debugger.

The cross-platform stacks have their own shortcuts to a device: `npx cap run ios` for Capacitor, and development
builds from EAS Build for Expo (see [[capacitor-expo-swift]]).

## Gotchas

- **Seven days, three apps.** On a free account an app you installed last week stops launching. Rebuild it, or join
  the program.
- **Bundle IDs are global.** Xcode refuses to set up signing with a bundle ID someone else already registered, so
  pick something with your own name or domain in it.
- **The phone can be too new.** Xcode has to support the iOS version on the device. Update Xcode when the phone gets a
  major update, and remember that Xcode 27 debugs only devices on iOS 17 or later.
- **Developer Mode is per device.** A new phone, or a phone that was erased, needs it turned on again.
- **Installed is not working.** `devicectl` proves the app is on the phone and launched. The feature still needs
  someone to use it.

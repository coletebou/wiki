Most Mac users end up choosing between two mice: Logitech's MX Master, built for long days of work, and Apple's Magic
Mouse, built to look like it belongs next to a Mac. Cole has used both and has a verdict on each. The MX Master is the
one on the desk.

## The Logitech MX Master

<ColesTake>

The MX Master is the all-time GOAT.

</ColesTake>

The MX Master line is Logitech's flagship productivity mouse, a popular pick for long days at a desk. The current model is the MX Master 4 (<Price id="mx-master" />). What makes the line good:

- **The MagSpeed wheel.** An electromagnetic scroll wheel that switches between a precise, clicky ratchet and a
  free-spinning mode Logitech rates at 1,000 lines per second. A long log, a transcript or a big file flies by with one
  flick, then the ratchet comes back for line-by-line work.
- **A thumb wheel.** A second wheel under your thumb scrolls sideways, through wide tables, timelines and code that
  runs off the screen.
- **A shape for a whole day.** A sculpted, right-handed body with a thumb rest, so the hand rests on the mouse instead
  of gripping it.
- **A sensor that works anywhere.** 8,000 DPI tracking that works on almost any surface, glass included.
- **Several computers, one mouse.** It pairs with more than one device and switches between them with a button, the
  same idea as the tri-mode [[mechanical-keyboard|keyboard]], over Bluetooth or a USB receiver.
- **Buttons per app.** Logitech's Options+ app remaps the buttons and gestures, differently for each app if you like.

The MX Master 4 adds a haptic panel under the thumb, which taps back on actions like snapping a window into place, and
an "Actions Ring" of shortcuts that opens at the cursor.

## Apple's Magic Mouse

<ColesTake>

The Magic Mouse is kinda sick and kinda sucks.

</ColesTake>

The Magic Mouse (<Price id="magic-mouse" />) has no wheel and no visible buttons: its whole top is a Multi-Touch
surface. That is the "sick" side, the reason people love it. You scroll in any direction by sliding a finger, swipe
between pages with one finger and between full-screen apps with two, and pairing it is as simple as plugging it into
the Mac once.

The "sucks" side is just as well known:

- **The charging port is on the bottom.** The 2024 model moved to USB-C but kept the port underneath, so you cannot
  use it while it charges.
- **It is flat.** The low, smooth shell is beautiful and not built for hours of gripping.
- **No wheel and no extra buttons.** No middle click, no thumb buttons, no free-spinning scroll for a long file.

For a lot of people the answer is a Magic Trackpad for gestures and a wheel mouse for work.

## Scroll settings that matter

A mouse wheel and a trackpad scroll differently, and Cole's setup tunes for that in three places:

- **Mos.** Cole's [[homebrew|Brewfile]] installs [Mos](https://github.com/Caldis/Mos), a free menu bar app that turns
  a wheel's notched steps into smooth scrolling on macOS and lets the mouse's scroll direction differ from the
  trackpad's. macOS ties the two together with a single "natural scrolling" switch.
- **The terminal.** Cole's [[ghostty|Ghostty]] config sets `mouse-scroll-multiplier = 0.3`, slowing wheel scrolling
  in the terminal well below Ghostty's default of 3 for a notched wheel.
- **tmux.** The [[tmux-keymap#the-mouse|tmux keymap]] deliberately inverts the wheel inside tmux, to match how
  [[termius]] on the phone sends scroll events, and enters scrollback with a flick of the wheel.

## Choosing

- **Long days of code, logs and agents:** an MX Master. The free-spinning wheel alone is worth it for scrolling
  through agent transcripts.
- **Gestures and a clean desk:** a Magic Mouse, or better, a Magic Trackpad.
- **Moving between computers:** a mouse that pairs with several and switches with a button, paired with a keyboard
  that does the same.

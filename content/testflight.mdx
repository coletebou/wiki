TestFlight is Apple's beta testing service. You upload a build to [[app-store-connect|App Store Connect]], put it in
a group, and testers install it through the free TestFlight app, from an email invitation or a public link. It is how
an iPhone app reaches anyone who is not plugged into your Mac, and it is Apple's answer to what Cole called "private
vs public beta": a private group of your own team that gets builds within minutes, and an external group, up to
10,000 people, that gets them after Apple's Beta App Review. It needs the paid Apple Developer Program
(<Price id="apple-developer-program" />); see [[ios-development]] for the rest of the path.

## Private and public betas

| | Internal testing (the private beta) | External testing (the public beta) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Up to 100 members of your App Store Connect team with the Account Holder, Admin, App Manager, Developer or Marketing role | Up to 10,000 people, invited by email or through a public link |
| Review | None | Beta App Review for the first build; later builds of the same version may not need a full review |
| How fast | As soon as the build finishes processing | After the build is approved |
| Delivery | Automatic distribution to the group can be switched on | You add builds to the group |
| Who sees whom | Testers are named team members | Public-link testers appear as anonymous |

A build can also be marked internal-only when you upload it, and such builds can go only to internal groups. That is
the right setting for anything with debug menus or unfinished features.

## What testers get

Testers install TestFlight from the App Store, accept the invitation, and then:

- Install every build you share with them, on up to 30 devices each.
- See your "What to Test" notes for each build.
- Send feedback by taking a screenshot inside your app and marking it up, or through the TestFlight app. If the app
  crashes, you get the crash report and the tester can add what they were doing.
- Keep each build for up to 90 days, after which it expires and stops launching.

TestFlight covers iPhone and iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Apple TV and Vision Pro apps. One catch for companies: Managed
Apple Accounts created in reserved domains cannot test builds.

## Setting it up

1. **Upload a build** from [[xcode|Xcode]], a script, or Expo's EAS Submit (see
   [[app-store-connect#getting-a-build-in|getting a build in]] and [[capacitor-expo-swift]]). Apple emails when it has
   finished processing.
2. **Fill in the test information**: a beta description, what to test, a feedback email, and, for external testing,
   sign-in details for the reviewer if your app needs an account.
3. **Create an internal group** in the TestFlight tab, add team members (they must accept the App Store Connect
   invitation first), and turn on "Enable automatic distribution" if every build should reach them. Builds made by
   Xcode Cloud always have to be added by hand.
4. **Create an external group**, add a build, and choose Submit Review. You can submit up to six builds for TestFlight
   review in 24 hours.
5. **Invite testers** by email or CSV import, or choose Create Public Link. A public link can be "Open to Anyone" or
   "Filter by Criteria" (device type and OS version), and can carry a tester limit anywhere from 1 to 10,000.

## How Cole runs betas

One of Cole's apps has run both kinds of beta, in that order. A private internal group of a handful of people tested
early builds, with testers invited into App Store Connect under the narrow Marketing role. When the app opened its
sign-up to the public, an external group with a public link joined it. From then on the release script did the
TestFlight work too: after each upload it waits for the build to become valid, adds it
to the public group and submits it for Beta App Review, so a merged fix turns into a reviewed public beta without
anyone opening a browser. The builds are numbered past one hundred by now.

Two lessons from that app shape how Cole treats a TestFlight build:

- **Uploaded is not tested.** A build that reached "Testing" in App Store Connect proves it compiled and installed,
  not that the new feature works. Features that depend on hardware, like a camera control on the volume buttons,
  only got tested at all on a physical iPhone through TestFlight, because the simulator cannot press those buttons.
- **The notes are a promise.** External release notes claim only what was verified on a device, because testers read
  them as what changed.

[[drover|Drover]] uses TestFlight for its own clients. Its manual `signed` workflow in
[[github-actions|GitHub Actions]] archives the iPhone app (with the [[apple-watch-apps|Apple Watch app]], the widgets
and the [[safari-extensions|Safari extension]] embedded inside it) and uploads it to TestFlight with an App Store
Connect API key; each app needed its App Store Connect record before the first upload worked.

For your own phone, internal TestFlight is the cable-free alternative to running from Xcode; the trade-offs are on
[[push-to-your-phone#testflight-the-cable-free-route|pushing straight to your phone]].

Private group first, public link second. Internal builds skip review and land in minutes, so that is where the dumb
stuff gets caught. The public link is for when it's ready for strangers.

</ColesTake>

## Gotchas

- **Builds expire after 90 days.** A beta that goes quiet for three months stops launching on testers' phones. Ship
  a fresh build before then, even an unchanged one.
- **Public links travel.** Anyone can forward the link, so testers can join without being invited. Use criteria, a
  tester limit, or disable the link once the group is full.
- **Public-link testers are anonymous.** You see their installs, sessions and crashes, but not their names or emails.
- **Friends need App Store Connect accounts for the private beta.** Internal testers are team members with roles. If
  that is too much, put them in an external group instead and accept the review.
- **Plan a day for the first external build.** Beta App Review is usually quick, but the first build of a new version
  gets the full review, and a rejection shows up under App Review in the sidebar.
- **Stop testing deliberately.** If you never expire a build, testers keep it even after that version goes live on
  the App Store.
- **A tester stuck on "No Builds Available"** with the right role, group and build was fixed, in Cole's case, by
  deleting and recreating the tester rather than by changing permissions.

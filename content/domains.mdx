A domain is the name everything else hangs on: the site, the API, the email addresses, the links inside an app. Cole
buys domains at Cloudflare's registrar, which sells them at cost, and runs every one of them as a zone on
[[cloudflare|Cloudflare]] DNS. A .com costs <Price id="domain-com" />, and most of the common endings cost about the
same.

## How domain pricing works

Three parties set the price of a domain:

- The **registry** runs the top-level domain (TLD), keeps the master list of names under it, and sets the wholesale
  price. Verisign runs .com; country-code TLDs such as .ai, .io and .uk are run for their countries and set their own
  rules.
- The **registrar** sells the name to you and renews it every year. Most registrars add a markup, and many discount
  the first year and make it back on renewals.
- **ICANN** adds a small fee to generic TLDs such as .com and .org.

Cloudflare's registrar charges exactly what the registry and ICANN charge it, with no markup, so the price you see is
the renewal price too. When a first year is cheaper there, as it is for several TLDs this month, the discount comes from
the registry itself. Names the registry classes as **premium** (short words, common terms) are priced one by one and
can cost far more, and names bought from another owner on the aftermarket cost whatever the seller asks.

## Which one to buy

- **.com is the gold standard.** People type it by default, autocomplete suggests it, and an email address at a .com
  looks normal to everyone. If the .com of your name is available at the standard price, buy it.
- **Match the ending to the audience when the .com is gone.** .ai says "AI product" before anyone reads a word;
  .dev and .app suit developer tools and apps, and come with HTTPS enforced; .org suits a community or a nonprofit; a
  country code suits a local business.
- **Short and spellable beats clever.** If you have to spell it out loud, it will be mistyped.
- **Buy the obvious neighbors of a name that matters**, such as the .com of your .ai, and redirect them with one rule
  on a proxied record.
- **A name for a side project can be anything.** Cole's own collection is a couple of .ai names for AI products, a
  handful of .coms, and a tail of novelty endings for side projects and jokes, nearly all of them on Cloudflare's
  free plan.

Cole said a domain "could be anywhere from $5 to $50 probably really generally... Really $5 to $30 most commonly."

## Buying and moving domains at Cloudflare

- **Search** at [domains.cloudflare.com](https://domains.cloudflare.com/); buying needs a Cloudflare account.
- **Nameservers are Cloudflare's.** A domain registered with Cloudflare must use Cloudflare's nameservers. To point it
  somewhere else, you would have to move the registration to another registrar first. (For Cole's setup that is the
  point: every domain's DNS lives at Cloudflare anyway.)
- **Privacy and safety are included.** WHOIS redaction is free, DNSSEC is one click, and domains are locked against
  transfer by default.
- **Transferring a domain in** takes about half an hour of work and up to ten days. The domain has to be active on
  Cloudflare DNS first, most transfers add a year to the registration, and ICANN blocks a transfer within 60 days of a
  registration, a previous transfer or a change to the registrant contact.
- **Verify the registrant email.** ICANN requires it; an unverified address puts the domain on hold.

## Domains in Cole's setup

A new domain gets the same treatment every time:

1. Register it at Cloudflare, or transfer it there.
2. Add the zone to the [[opentofu|OpenTofu]] code, usually to the shared root for small zones.
3. If it receives mail, turn on [[cloudflare-email-routing|Email Routing]]; if it sends none, publish the no-mail
   records (`MX 0 .`, `v=spf1 -all`, a DMARC reject policy), so nobody can send mail as it.
4. Point it at whatever serves it: a DNS-only record for a frontend on [[vercel|Vercel]], or a proxied record through
   a tunnel for a service on one of the [[servers]] (see [[cloudflare-front-door]]).
5. Turn on DNSSEC only together with the DS record at the registrar, and never turn it off while the DS record is still
   published, or the zone stops resolving.

The yearly renewals are on [[what-it-costs]], next to everything else.

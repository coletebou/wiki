This wiki is Cole Tebou's whole working setup written down in one place: the terminal, the agents, the infrastructure, the apps, the desk, and what all of it costs. It started as Cole's request for "a guide on how to be a beast", and it became a wiki because every piece connects to five others and deserves its own page.

Start with [[how-to-be-a-beast|How to Be a Beast]] for the tour, or browse the [Pinakes](/pinakes/), the catalogue of every page. This page is about the book itself.

## How to read it

Every page is a *folio*, a leaf of the codex, with a number like **f. 11r**. The front of the leaf (the *recto*, "r") carries the text. The back (the *verso*, "v") carries what a scholar wants after reading: which other folios cite this one, which it cites, the sources it rests on, when it was last emended, and how to emend it. Under the date of the last emendation the same day is written as a scribe of Leonardo's Florence would have dated it, counted back from the Kalends, Nones or Ides, with the year in Roman numerals.

- **The cabinet.** The frontispiece shows the ten tables as a cabinet of rolls (the Romans kept theirs in cupboards they called *armaria*): a pigeonhole for each table and a roll for every folio. Point at a roll for its tag, or press it to open the folio. A gold boss marks the eight most cited folios, a red tag the eight most lately emended.
- **The planisphere.** [The planisphere](/planisphere/) draws the whole library as a chart of the heavens: every folio is a star, drawn brighter the more folios cite it, and each table is a constellation, its stars joined in the order of its leaves. Point at a star and its citations are drawn across the chart, red to the folios it cites and blue from the folios that cite it. The stars stand in the same places on every build.
- **The Fasti.** [The Fasti](/fasti/) is the wiki's calendar of emendations. The Romans' *fasti* were a list of the days of the year, and on them each day carried a letter from A to H for its place in the eight-day market week. Here every folio stands under the day it was last emended, newest first, each day with its Roman date and its letter, counted on from A on the first of January without a break. Press <kbd>f</kbd> to open it.
- **Links carry tags.** Hover over any link, or focus it with the keyboard, and a small parchment tag swings down with the page's title, its table and a one-line summary. On a phone there is no hover, so a tap simply follows the link.
- **Seek anything.** Press <kbd>/</kbd> or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> on a Mac) anywhere to search every word of every folio. The search runs in your browser against an index built with the site, so it is fast and sends your query nowhere. Before you type, it offers the folios you read last.
- **Turn leaves from the keyboard.** <kbd>n</kbd> turns to the next leaf and <kbd>p</kbd> to the one before, <kbd>v</kbd> goes to the back of the leaf, <kbd>c</kbd> opens the catalogue, <kbd>m</kbd> the planisphere, <kbd>f</kbd> the fasti, <kbd>h</kbd> the frontispiece, and <kbd>l</kbd> lights the lamp. <kbd>?</kbd> lists them all. A study that reads the keyboard itself keeps its keys while you are in it.
- **The catchword.** At the foot of each folio's text stand the first words of the folio that follows, as binders had them written so that the leaves of a book were gathered in order. It is also the link to that folio.
- **The explicit.** Above it, in red, the text closes the way a scribe closed one: *explicit*, "here it ends", and the folio's number in Roman numerals. It is ornament, so screen readers skip it.
- **A card for every folio.** A link to a folio, posted anywhere, shows that folio's own card: its table and number, its title in the inscription capitals, the start of its summary, and its table's device, drawn when the site is built.
- **The ribbon.** Leave a long folio part read and a ribbon is laid at the last heading you read past. Come back to the folio and it offers to be taken up there. Read it to the end and the ribbon comes out. This too is kept in your browser and nowhere else.
- **Lately on your table.** The frontispiece lists the folios you opened last. The list is kept in your browser and nowhere else, and one press clears it.
- **Sortes.** Press <kbd>s</kbd>, or follow the line under the cabinet, and the library opens at a folio drawn by lot, the way Romans opened Virgil at random for counsel (the *sortes Vergilianae*).
- **Lamplight.** The oil lamp in the top bar is the theme switch. Light it and the leaf goes dark for reading at night, with the lamp's own light on the head of the page; put it out for daylight. Without a choice of your own, the site follows your system setting.
- **Hold a mirror to the margin.** On a wide screen the outer margin carries lines from Leonardo's notebooks in his mirror writing. Point at one and it turns to face you, and its tag gives the English.
- **Studies in ink.** Some folios hold interactive drawings: the [[tmux-keymap|tmux keymap]], the [[agent-fleet|agent fleet]], [[parallel-worktrees|worktree fan-out]], the [[observability|observability path]], the [[cloudflare-front-door|Cloudflare front door]] and the [[what-it-costs|cost calculator]]. Click, tap or tab through them.

## Why it looks like this

Cole asked for a wiki that looks "like Leonardo da Vinci's journals and the Library of Alexandria". Those two are a good match for what this wiki is: a working notebook that is also a catalogue.

**Leonardo's notebooks** are the model for the page. He filled thousands of sheets with machines, water studies, anatomy and lists, in brown iron-gall ink with red chalk (*sanguine*) for drawing, and wrote his notes right to left in mirror script. So the text here is set in iron-gall brown on parchment, the accents are red chalk, the diagrams are hatched ink sketches, and the outer margin carries lines from his notebooks in mirror writing. That marginalia is ornament only: it is hidden from screen readers and never holds anything you need to read.

**The man on the frontispiece** is Leonardo's own drawing: his study of the body's proportions after Vitruvius, made in pen and ink about 1490 and kept at the Gallerie dell'Accademia in Venice. His lines were traced into vector ink from Luc Viatour's photograph of the sheet (credit: Luc Viatour / [Lucnix.be](https://lucnix.be)), so the drawing takes the colour of the page's ink, brown by day and pale by lamplight, and stays sharp at any size. The handwriting above and below the figure is Leonardo's too, in his mirror hand. Where the photograph shows the circle too faint to follow, about a fifteenth of its length, the tracing carries the line across. The four machines in the margins round him are his as well, traced the same way from his notebooks: a game of circles in a hexagon from the Codex Atlanticus (f. 272v), the flying machine a man stands in from Manuscript B, the wheels in mesh of a winch (Codex Atlanticus f. 21r), and from the same sheet the spit that the hot air rising from a fire turns by itself, a machine that runs on its own. The outer margin of every folio has one of his machines too, each table its own: the aerial screw for the Shell, the self-turning spit for the Automata, the flying machine for the Atelier, a great flywheel press for the Works. And each of the studies on the frontispiece stands under one of his drawings for its motto: the keyboard of his viola organista for the keys, the self-turning spit for the machines, a press whose arms all run from one spindle for the branches, water running past an obstacle for the rivers, a door's lock for the gates, and a game of equal areas for what it costs. Inside the studies themselves, a small sketch stands in an empty corner of each view's sheet, after its motto. Three of those are his, the winch's wheels, the water and the lock; the hourglass, the sealed letter, the torch, the moon, the knot, the shield and the road were drawn by an image model in the manner of his pen and traced the same way.

**The name above him** is set in the capitals Luca Pacioli built with compass and ruler for *De divina proportione*, printed in Venice in 1509, the book whose solids Leonardo drew. Each letter was traced from the woodcut in two layers, the letter and the circles and ruled lines that made it, and both take the page's ink: the letters at full strength, their construction lighter behind them. Pacioli's alphabet, like every Roman inscription, has no U, so the name is cut as a Roman would have cut it: CODEX TEBOV. The masthead of every page has the same letters without their geometry, and the icon in a browser's tab is his C.

**The book itself** is the kind Leonardo's Florence made. Each folio is a leaf of vellum: mottled as skin is, cut with a wavering edge, pricked and ruled before it was written on, and lying on a stone reading table with the leaves still to be read stacked under it. The text of each folio opens on one of Pacioli's capitals, the same alphabet as the name on the frontispiece, standing in its square with the compass work that built it drawn lighter behind. Pacioli has no J, U or W, and neither did the Roman inscriptions he measured: a folio that opens on a U opens on a V, as a printer of 1509 would have set it, and a W is two V's crossed, which is where the letter came from. The labels in red are *rubrics*, the scribe's red ink. And Cole's own opinions are marked the way readers have marked passages since the twelfth century, with a *manicule*, a small pointing hand in the margin.

**The Library of Alexandria** is the model for the catalogue. Its first great librarian-cataloguer, Callimachus of Cyrene, wrote the *Pinakes* ("tables"), sorting the library's scrolls by subject and recording for each work its title, its opening words (the *incipit*) and its length in lines (*stichoi*). The [Pinakes](/pinakes/) of this wiki does the same for every folio: ten tables, each entry with its incipit and its length in stichoi, counted the Alexandrian way at about 36 letters to the line. Each scroll in Alexandria also carried a small parchment tag, a *sillybos*, naming the work so nobody had to unroll it to find out. Those are the tags that hang from every link here.

**The catalogue's roll.** The Pinakes is not written on a leaf of the codex but on a roll of papyrus, as the books of that library were. Papyrus was made of two layers of strips pressed crosswise, and a roll was many such sheets pasted end to end, so the sheet here shows its fibres both ways, a faint lap at each join, and sides that fray; it is wound on a rod at head and foot, with its title tag hung from it. It opens downward, as a *rotulus* does, because a screen is read that way. Between one table and the next stands a forked *paragraphos*, the stroke a scribe drew at the start of a line where the subject changed. At the foot a *coronis* marks the end of the work, and under it the count of lines is written in Attic numerals, the old acrophonic numbers of Athens in which the line totals of the classical authors have come down to us. The catalogue can also be narrowed: write a word on the line under the tables' names and only the folios whose title, other names or summary hold it remain.

**Each table has its device,** a small drawing that stands for it as a printer's or a guild's device stood for theirs: a counting frame for the Desk, a scallop for the Shell, wheels in mesh for the Automata, dividers for the Workshop, an aqueduct for the Works, an armillary sphere for the Observatory, a wing for the Atelier, a coin for the Mint, a balance for the Ledger and an open book for the Library. Each was first drawn by an image model asked for a single object in Leonardo's pen, bold enough to read when printed very small, and then traced into vector ink the same way as his own sketches, so it takes the page's ink in either light. Leonardo drew none of them; they are drawn after his manner, and marked so here. The device stands at the head of its table in the catalogue and, small, before the table's name on every folio of it.

**The page that is not there** is a leaf that was in the fire. Its corner is burnt away, and by lamplight the edge is still alight.

Readability comes first. Body text is set in EB Garamond at a real size with more than 13:1 contrast in both themes, every colour that text is set in holds at least 4.5:1 (a test in the repository keeps it so), the layout falls to a single column on a phone, and nothing you need depends on hover alone.

## How it is built

The site is static: plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, built ahead of time and served from a CDN.

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| [Astro](https://astro.build) with MDX | Turns each folio (one `.mdx` file in `content/`) into a page. MDX is Markdown that can hold components, which is how the diagrams and the price tags get in. |
| Wiki links | `[[slug]]`, `[[slug\|label]]` and `[[slug#section\|label]]` link folios by name, title or alias. A link to a folio that does not exist fails the build. |
| Backlinks | The build reads every link in every folio, so the back of each leaf can list who cites it. |
| [Pagefind](https://pagefind.app) | Indexes every word after the build and searches it in the browser. |
| Prices | Every price on the site comes from one data file, with the date it was read and a link to the vendor's own page. See [[what-it-costs]]. |
| Fonts | EB Garamond for the text, Cinzel for the inscriptions, Italianno for Leonardo's hand, JetBrains Mono for code. All under the SIL Open Font License and served from this site, never a third-party font CDN. |
| Materials and illumination | The vellum, the stone, the papyrus, the outlines of the leaf and the roll, the burnt edge and the planisphere are drawn by code when the site is built, a few lines of SVG each. The drawings are traced files: Leonardo's man and his machines from photographs of his sheets, Pacioli's capitals from a scan of the 1509 woodcuts, and the ten devices, seven of the studies' corner sketches and the burnt scroll on the page for a lost address from an image model's pen drawings. Each is a mask the page's ink shows through, never a picture with its own colour. |
| Feed | The thirty folios most lately emended are published as an [Atom feed](/emendations.xml), for a feed reader. |

The site is hosted on [[vercel|Vercel]], and its name, `wiki.coletebou.com`, is a DNS record at [[cloudflare|Cloudflare]] like every other domain Cole runs. It is one of the editions of coletebou.com, beside the Almanac, the Plan Set and the Terminal: the switcher at the foot of every page moves between them.

## How it is kept

The source lives in a public GitHub repository, [coletebou/wiki](https://github.com/coletebou/wiki). Nothing reaches the site except through a pull request, and every pull request runs the same [[github-actions|GitHub Actions]] checks:

1. **build**: the whole site builds, every wiki link resolves, every page's frontmatter is valid, every price id exists.
2. **links**: every internal link and section anchor in the built site points at something real.
3. **gitleaks**: no credential is in the change.
4. **public-safety**: nothing that must stay private is published. The rules are written down in [[wiki-mcp#the-public-safety-rules|the public-safety rules]].

Agents edit the wiki too. Any agent with the wiki's [[mcp|MCP]] server can search and read every folio, follow backlinks, and propose an edit. A proposed edit becomes a branch, a commit and a pull request labelled `agent-edit`, opened with the agent's own GitHub credentials. When all four checks pass on an `agent-edit` pull request that Cole's own account opened from a branch of this repository, automation squash-merges it and the site redeploys. Anything else waits for a human. [[wiki-mcp|Editing the wiki with agents]] has the details and the setup for [[claude-code|Claude Code]] and [[codex|Codex]].

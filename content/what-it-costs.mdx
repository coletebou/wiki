Most of this setup is free. The bill comes from two places: the AI subscriptions that run the agents, and a short list
of plans paid for because a free tier stopped being enough. This page lists every line item with its price as read from
the vendor's own page on October 28, 2026, in three setups, from a first project to Cole's own.

<Diagram name="cost-calculator" />

The calculator starts from the same three setups as the tables below. Pick one, change the quantities (seats, domains,
gigabytes, servers), switch items on or off, and it adds up a monthly and a yearly total from the same price list that
every page of this wiki uses. Yearly prices are spread over twelve months, usage prices are multiplied out, and one-time
purchases are kept apart from the monthly figure.

## How prices work on this wiki

Every price on every page is a tag, not typed text. It prints the amount, the day it was read and a link to the page it
was read on, and all of them come from one data file. When a vendor changes a price, that file changes and every page
follows. A price that could not be confirmed on the vendor's own page says *unverified* next to it. Agents that edit
this wiki ([[wiki-mcp]]) follow the same rule: a price is a tag or it is not on the page.

Read the unit and the billing period in each one. Several prices here hold only when paid a year at a time (Cloudflare
Pro, Datadog Pro, Sentry Team, Termius Pro), some are per seat or per user, some per zone or per host, and storage is
per gigabyte per month. Taxes are not included.

## What is worth paying for

**Free tiers that are genuinely enough** for one person: Cloudflare Free for nearly every domain, Tailscale Personal
for personal use, Vercel Hobby for personal and non-commercial projects, GitHub Free (and free Actions on public
repositories), Sentry Developer, Langfuse Hobby or self-hosted Langfuse, Datadog's free tier for up to five hosts, and
OpenTofu.

**Plans to pay for when you hit the limit, not before:**

- Cloudflare Pro, on the zone that takes production traffic, when five firewall rules and one ten-second rate limit are
  not enough, or when bot protection has to make exceptions for your own clients.
- Vercel Pro, when a project makes money, when more than one person commits, or when the deployment volume outgrows
  Hobby.
- Tailscale's paid plans, for business use or for more groups in the access rules.
- Sentry Team, for more than one user or for third-party integrations.

**Costs that look free and are not:**

- **macOS minutes in CI.** Free on public repositories, about ten times a Linux minute on private ones. An automated
  commit that triggers macOS jobs on every push can cost more than every plan on this page; see
  [[github-actions#how-a-ci-bill-runs-away|how a CI bill runs away]].
- **Renewals after a first-year discount.** Compare domain renewals, not first years.
- **Logs.** Datadog bills logs per gigabyte ingested and per million events indexed, separately from hosts.
- **Requests and retrievals on S3.** Storage is cheap; requests, retrievals and the Glacier minimum durations are where
  surprises come from.

## Guardrails

A bill is easier to keep than to fix. Cole's are all set up once and then left alone:

- **AWS Budgets** emails at 85% and 100% of the month's budget and when the forecast passes 100%, and **Cost Anomaly
  Detection** flags any service whose spend jumps. Both are part of the AWS organization in [[opentofu|OpenTofu]].
- **A GitHub Actions budget** stops hosted jobs in private repositories when it is reached.
- **Short-lived machines** carry a hard instance cap and their own monthly budget (see
  [[servers#burst-capacity|burst capacity]]).
- **Langfuse** prices every model call, including those on flat subscriptions, at API rates, which is how Cole checks
  whether a plan is still worth more than it costs (see [[langfuse#cost-analysis|cost analysis]]).

## Reading your own bill

The tables above are list prices. Your real numbers are in each vendor's own usage page, and it is worth reading them
once a month:

- **AWS**: Cost Explorer, grouped by service, shows what S3, EC2 and the rest really cost, including the requests and
  data transfer that list prices leave out.
- **GitHub**: the billing usage report breaks Actions down by repository and runner type, which is where a runaway
  workflow shows up first.
- **Vercel**: the usage tab shows how close each resource is to the plan's included amounts.
- **Cloudflare**: billing lists each zone's plan and any add-ons, so a forgotten Pro zone does not hide.
- **Langfuse**: its cost dashboards show what the model calls would cost at API prices, per project, model and session,
  which is the fair way to compare a subscription with paying per token.
